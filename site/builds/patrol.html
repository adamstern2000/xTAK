Patrol
node.

The everyday node. A battery-powered Raspberry Pi 4B that runs netTAK — Standalone out of the box, or full Mesh with the HaLow radio — plus offline maps, GPS timing, and an external-antenna option. Sized to move with a team and keep a self-contained TAK network in your pack.

FEATURES

What the
Patrol gives you.

  • Battery-powered, all-day. A UPS power board and a 10,000 mAh cell run the node off-grid, with live battery state right in the adminTAK panel.
  • GPS timing & position. Add the GPS module and the node disciplines the cluster clock without internet — and reports its own position to your ATAK map.
  • Reach when you need it. Drop in the high-gain USB Wi-Fi adapter and external antennas for client coverage well beyond the built-in radio.
  • Field-sealed I/O. Panel-mount Ethernet and antenna bulkheads bring connectors to the outside of the case without breaking the seal.
  • One-button operation. A single momentary button for clean power-up and safe shutdown — no keyboard, no console.
  • HaLow mesh backhaul. The Wi-Fi HaLow radio links Patrol nodes into a self-healing mesh across long distances.

BUILD GUIDE

Putting it together.

Most of the Patrol build is plug-together. A few steps need care — heat-set inserts, a little soldering, and wiring the GPS. Step-by-step photos and instructions for each are on the way.

  1. Set the heat-set inserts. Melt the M3 (cover) and mounting inserts into the printed case with a soldering iron.
  2. Solder the power button. Wire the 12 mm momentary button to the PiSugar power header.
  3. Wire the GPS module (optional). Jumper the GPS to the Pi's GPIO UART pins.
  4. Mount the panel connectors. Fit the RJ45 bulkhead and antenna pigtails to the case wall.
  5. Lay out the internals. Seat the Pi, PiSugar, battery, and HaLow radio; route USB-C and Ethernet.
